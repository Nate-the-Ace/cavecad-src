Find, switch and restyle layers in the Layer Manager

A worked cave carries three hundred layers. The Layer Manager is the panel for living with that. View > Layer Manager, or type gm.

It sits beside CaveCAD's plain Layer List, which is untouched and still there — the flat alphabetical view is one click away when you want it.

The table

Eight columns, in the order a CAD layer table conventionally uses, because that is the table most cavers already know:

  • Layer — the name, and the group tree
  • On — shown or hidden
  • Freeze — frozen out of regeneration
  • Lock — visible but not editable
  • Plot — whether it prints
  • Color — swatch and hex
  • Linetype — solid, dashed, and the rest
  • Lineweight — printed thickness

On and Freeze are separate, which they are not in the plain Layer List. Switching a layer off hides it; freezing it also takes it out of regeneration, which is what you want for the hundred-odd generated layers while you work.

Eight columns will not fit a narrow palette. Right-click the header to tick the ones you want, and CaveCAD remembers.

Changing things

  1. Click a switch — On, Freeze, Lock, Plot — to toggle it.
  2. Click Color, Linetype or Lineweight for a picker. It opens at the cell, hanging below it, or above it when the row is near the bottom of the screen. Click anywhere else to dismiss it having changed nothing.

Select several layers and the edit applies to all of them. That is the point of the table: pick eight layers, click one Freeze box, all eight freeze. Clicking a row that is not in the selection moves the highlight there and edits only that one — so what is highlighted is always what you are about to change, and the picker names it across its top.

A group row counts as everything under it. The whole edit is one undo, however many layers it touched.

A mixed set is decided once rather than flipped row by row: if any of them is on, they all go off. Otherwise a mixed selection would only ever become a differently mixed one.

The pickers

  • Color — a grid: the everyday eight, a column per hue and a row per shade, the greys, the last colour you mixed by hand, and Custom… for the full picker.
  • Linetype and Lineweight — a list that draws what it is offering, so you pick a dash pattern or a thickness by looking at it rather than by reading its name.

Isolating a layer

Right-click a layer and pick Isolate to hide everything else. Right-click a multi-selection to isolate the lot, or a group row to isolate the group.

While something is isolated, the only entry is Unisolate, named after whatever is isolated — not after the row you right-clicked. You cannot isolate a second time. That is on purpose: isolating again would take its picture of a cave that is already hidden and write it over the only record of how the cave really looked, and your arrangement would be gone with nothing to undo.

Unisolate does not mean "show all layers", which would invent an arrangement you never chose. Every layer goes back to the colour, linetype, lineweight and four switches it actually had — so a layer that was hidden before you isolated is hidden again afterwards.

The isolated row is shown in bold, so a cave down to one layer says why.

The isolated layer is shown even if it was switched off, and becomes the current layer. A layer you create while isolated stays visible: you just made it, so you are looking at it. Applying a layer state ends the isolation — the state is the arrangement you have just asked for, and an Unisolate left armed would undo your choice later on its own.

It is remembered in the drawing. Save while isolated and the file itself has every layer hidden, so the way back has to travel with it: reopen and Unisolate is waiting. An older CaveCAD will not know about it and can only fall back to Show All Layers.

Groups

Layers are filed into groups, and groups nest one level:

  • Plan — Passage, Floor, Formations, Water, Geology & Finds, Notes
  • Profile, Cross Sections — each view's own hand-traced ink, its notes and water included
  • Scans & Basemap — sketch scans, aerial imagery, surface contours
  • Control — everything the suite generates, in all three views at once
  • Sheet — border, title block, legend, scale bar, north arrow, and layer 0
  • Trips — a row per survey trip, holding the layers of the runs it surveyed

Six rows at the top level, so the panel opens as a short list you can read rather than a wall. A group's eye and lock act on everything under it and show mixed when its members disagree.

A new cave map arrives already filed — the template carries the arrangement. An older drawing gets it from Repair Drawing, whose layer-groups pass files anything unfiled. That pass only ever adds: a group you made and a layer you filed by hand are both left alone.

Right-click for New Group, New Group Inside, Rename, Move to Top Level, Delete Group, and Add to Group for the selected layers. Deleting a group never deletes a layer, and never deletes the groups nested inside it — they move up.

A layer can be in more than one group. Add to Group on a layer that is already filed adds a second home rather than moving it, and the layer then appears under both — useful for a group like Trip 3 or Check before plotting that cuts across the standard six. Switching it in one place switches the layer, so both rows change together; Remove from takes away that one membership and leaves the others. The default filing never does this: it puts each layer in exactly one group, and the second one is yours to make.

Trip groups

Trips is a group of its own, with a row per survey trip, filled in for you.

A trip owns no layers — provenance rides on the entities themselves, and splitting layers by trip would cut a wall in half the moment it was continued on a later trip. What a trip does own is survey runs, and a run has real layers: the per-run profile layers. So Trip 3 means the layers of the runs surveyed on trip 3, which is what you mean when you say it — show me what I drew that day.

It is an extra membership, never a move: a layer stays in Profile and gains its trip. A run continued the following weekend appears under both trips, because that is the truth about the layer.

The group is derived, not assigned once, so a trip drawn before anything was traced in its band starts empty and fills in. Repair Drawing re-derives them all; drawing a page, importing a survey, and correcting a trip's name each keep them current on their own. A trip with no name is listed by its number alone.

Expand All and Collapse All are at the top of the same menu, and what you leave open is remembered.

Ungrouped is last and holds whatever is in no group — layers that appeared after the filing. You can rename that row to whatever it actually means in your cave.

The filter

The box at the top narrows the list as you type. Plain text matches anywhere in a name, so scan finds the scan layers.

Type a wildcard and it becomes a pattern, anchored to the whole name:

  • CTRL-* — everything generated
  • *scan* — anywhere in the name
  • CTRL-LRUD-WALL-????? — ? is exactly one character, so this finds RIGHT and not LEFT
  • WATER-*,FORMATIONS-* — a comma separates alternatives

A search flattens the list. While the box has something in it the groups go away and you get a plain list of the layers that matched, so you can select the lot and work on them rather than hunting them down eleven groups. A layer filed in two groups still appears once. Clear the box and the groups come back, collapsed exactly as you left them.

Typing a group's name brings in everything filed in it, so passage lists the passage layers.

Layer states

A state is a saved picture of every layer setting: on, frozen, locked, plottable, colour, linetype and lineweight. Not just what is visible.

The button at the foot of the panel picks one. Two come with every drawing:

  • Tracing — scans and survey control on, page furniture out of the way
  • Plot ready — scans and control off, the page back

Those two carry visibility only, on purpose, so applying one cannot put your colours back to the day the template was built. A state you save captures everything, because that is what saving one means.

+ saves the drawing as it stands under a new name, ↻ overwrites the one showing, − deletes it. The ⋯ button holds Rename State, Export States and Import States.

A layer that did not exist when the state was saved is left alone when it is applied. Nothing is guessed at.

Carrying states to another cave

Export writes a .clas file — CaveCAD's own, not the .las layer states other CAD packages write, and the two do not read each other. Import merges: a state of the same name is replaced, everything else is left, so re-importing what you just exported changes nothing.

Layers the other cave does not have are dropped, and the report says how many, because carrying one cave's states to another is a normal thing to do and a quiet partial result would not be.

States never carry groups. Importing states into a cave you have arranged by hand cannot disturb that arrangement.

Worth knowing

  • Group and state names cannot contain |, which is what separates them internally.
  • Groups and states are stored in the drawing, so they travel with the file.
  • Filing a layer into a group is not on the undo stack — it changes no geometry, and an undo of a drawing edit that silently unfiled a layer would be the more surprising of the two.
  • Delete a layer somewhere else and it leaves its groups and states quietly; the panel notices next time it reads them.
