Check Map

Read the finished map back and say what is missing or wrong — and why it matters. chk


The findings list, the why box under it, and Show Me / Check Again.


Check Again reads the map, then clicking a finding explains it.

When you reach for it

Before every sheet, and before handing a cave to anyone. Someone drawing their first map does not know what they have left out; that is the whole difficulty.

Steps

  1. Cave Survey > Check Map, then Check Again.
  2. Click a finding: the drawing pans to it at the magnification you are already working at, so walking down the list walks the cave under a steady zoom.
  3. Read why it matters. Press Show Me to reframe close up.
  4. Fix it, press Check Again, watch it leave the list. It is meant to be worked down to nothing.
  5. Right-click a finding for Ignore This — it hides that one finding and nothing else.

What it looks for

Fourteen checks: no scale bar, no north arrow, a title block that does not say who surveyed it or when, symbols used with no legend, a symbol on the wrong layer, work stranded on layer 0 or on a layer switched off, two wall lines that nearly meet but leave a hole, linework nowhere near any station, a breakdown boundary left open, a cross section tied to no station, a shaped line whose ornament has drifted, a floor ledge whose hachures the nearest floor levels say are on the high side, and a loop closure over the threshold worth questioning.

When it refuses

  • It changes nothing. A finding may be a decision rather than a mistake; this is the tool that explains, not the one that repairs.
  • A finding with nowhere to pan to — a missing scale bar is nowhere in particular — moves the view not at all.
