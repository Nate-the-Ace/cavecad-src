Vertical caves: how to tell CaveCAD

You don't. There is no switch marked this cave is vertical — the survey says so, and everything that follows reads it from the numbers.

That is deliberate. A flag can be forgotten, or set on the wrong cave, or disagree with the data underneath it; and a cave is rarely all one thing. Most long horizontal caves have vertical in them, and a pit usually has a crawl at the bottom. What matters is that each shot is recorded honestly. Get the notes right and the pitches, the labels, the elevation and the adjustment all follow.

Recording a pitch

A plumbed drop is a shot with the clino at 90 — written -90, or DOWN, or -V, and +90 / UP for one going up — and no bearing at all. Leave the compass column blank, or write a dash. That is not laziness, it is the measurement: hanging on a rope there is nothing to sight along, the needle swings, and a number written there would be a formality. CaveCAD records the absence, writes it back out as a dash, and refuses to let anything downstream read it as a bearing of north.

If the shot is not plumb, it needs a bearing. A dash on anything short of a true plumb is refused, and you are told which line — at 85° a 100 ft tape still swings nearly 9 ft across the map and there is no honest direction to swing it in. Record the clino as 90 if it really was plumb; otherwise read the compass.

The line: 85 degrees

At and past 85° from horizontal, a shot is a pitch. That one number decides everything: whether a bearing on that leg is treated as evidence of direction, whether the leg counts as a way out of its stations, how the walls at its foot are drawn, and whether the adjustment treats it as hanging from gravity or sighted with a compass. Under 85° it is steep passage and behaves like any other shot.

A near-plumb shot is also flagged in Check Map — not because it is wrong, but because it is a classic place for a sign to get flipped.

LRUD on a pitch

  • UP at the foot of a drop is open. Write P. The ceiling there is the pitch, and there is nothing to measure. A blank means "nobody looked", which is a different claim.
  • Left and right at a pit foot are drawn across the passage you are about to walk, not perpendicular to a compass reading nobody took. Record them as you always would.
  • Record the lip. The station at the head of a drop is the one a map most wants a width for, and the easiest to skip with your hands full.
  • The first station has no shot arriving at it, so its walls live in the trip's own first row. At a sinkhole rim, fill it in — otherwise the entrance draws with no width at all.

Elevation

Give the entrance its real elevation, not zero. In a 380 ft pit the datum trap is not subtle: see Elevation, and the datum trap.

What CaveCAD does with all that

  • Finds every drop and aven and labels it where it hangs from, in the plan and in the elevation — P 187 ft (62 + 125). In plan a pitch has no size at all, so without the label the deepest thing in the cave draws as a numbered dot.
  • A rebelayed drop is one pitch, because that is what a caver calls it. The total comes first, the rope sections follow in brackets. A passage leading off the rebelay ledge does not split it — it is still 187 ft of air.
  • An aven is decided by the cave, not by which end you started the tape. If nothing carries on at the top, it is an aven; push it into passage and it becomes a pitch down from that passage.
  • Anything under 10 ft gets no label. A plumbed step is not a pitch.
  • Nothing invents a bearing. A pitch contributes no way out of its stations, a section on a pitch is not silently oriented north, and a wall is never drawn in a direction nobody sighted.
  • A plumbed leg is weighted by gravity in the loop adjustment rather than by a compass it never used — so misclosure is not poured into the one line on the drawing whose true attitude a reader already knows.

A cave is rarely all one thing

Most long horizontal caves have vertical in them and most pits have a crawl at the bottom, so the engine never asks which kind you have — it decides per piece of cave, from that piece's own geometry. A chunked elevation cuts the cave at its pitches, then measures each piece: one that would fold back behind itself if flattened onto a plane is unrolled so it reads end to end, and one that would not is left projected, at true position. A branch that unrolling cannot carry becomes a piece of its own rather than being dropped.

So the same command, with nothing set differently, unrolls the 2400 ft trunk of a horizontal cave and stacks the ropes of a pit — in the same drawing if the cave has both.

Choosing the view

Cave Survey > Generate Profile asks which elevation to draw and remembers the answer.

Chunked is the one that handles a cave with both kinds of passage in it, because it is the only one that stops treating the cave as one thing. Take it unless you have a reason not to.

Extended unrolls the whole cave on one rule: right for a long horizontal cave that wanders, wrong for anything with rope in it, because the arrangement of the shafts — which in a pit is the cave — is the first thing it throws away, and the bands it stacks are pushed off true depth to stop them overprinting.

Projected keeps the whole cave in one piece at true position: right for a pit, and it draws passages on top of each other wherever the cave really does.

See Generate Profile.

Rigging

Bolts, Y-hangs, rebelays, deviations, natural anchors, rope, cable ladder and traverse lines are in the Rigging category of the Symbol Palette, all on one layer so a map printed for someone who is not rigging the cave turns the whole topo off in one click. Add your own there too.

A pitch length belongs in a note beside the rope, not baked into the symbol: re-rig the pitch and the note changes while the symbol stays true.

Rock outside the walls

Toggle Wall Edging dresses the elevation as well as the plan, which matters here because on a pit map the elevation is the view you read. It works on traced walls, so trace some with Feature Trace first — on a freshly imported survey there is nothing for it to dress and it says so. See Shaped Lines.

Settings worth knowing

  • CaveSurvey/ProfileMode — extended, projected or chunked. Set by Generate Profile.
  • CaveSurvey/ProjectionAzimuth — the plane a projected elevation is flattened onto, or blank for the cave's own longest direction.
  • CaveSurvey/PitchLabels — turn the generated depth labels off if you are lettering by hand.
