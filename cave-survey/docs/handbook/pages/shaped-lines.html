Shaped Lines

Cave-map line symbology as real, self-maintaining geometry. shl


Drag along an edge: a floor ledge's hachures, then a rimstone dam. The drag is animated; the lines are real.

When you reach for it

For the six NSS line symbols: Floor Ledge (lgf), Ceiling Ledge (lgc), Pit (pte), Flowstone (fst), Rimstone Dam (rst), Slope (slp). They are drawn from the Shaped Lines group in the Feature Trace panel.

Steps

  1. Arm the symbol, then drag along the edge.
  2. Release, and the feature follows your cursor until you click the side the ornament goes on — hachures on the down side, scallops bowing downslope, slope fans splaying downhill.
  3. Edit the line afterwards with QCAD's own tools; the ornament follows.
  4. A stroke continuing your last shaped line of the same style grows it, spine and ornament together, on one undo, with the side inherited.

The same buttons work in both views: a stroke inside the elevation's band boxes lands on the PROFILE- twin layers automatically.

The other three commands

  • shl itself is Decorate Selection: dress any existing line, polyline, arc, circle or spline the same way.
  • Flip Shaped Side (shf) mirrors the ornament on a line already drawn.
  • Sync Shaped Lines (shs) rebuilds the ornament by hand.

Rock outside the walls

Toggle Wall Edging (wed) is a switch: stone glyphs outside every surveyed wall, or take them all away again. It rides the drawing, so it survives a save.

Which side is outside is not a guess. The direction from a point on a wall to the nearest survey station is the direction of the cave, so outside is the other way — decided per glyph, not per wall, because one wall can run out of open passage into a fin between two passages and the answer changes along it. A wall with cave on both faces has no outside and is left bare rather than having stone drawn into the passage next door.

Where the nearest station lies nearly along the wall it says almost nothing about across, and that glyph takes the answer of the nearest confident one either side of it. On long wandering passage a quarter of the positions are in that state, so it is worth knowing they follow the wall rather than a single vote for the whole polyline.

It dresses the plan and the elevation. On a pit map the elevation is the view you actually read, so it edges PROFILE-WALLS-SURVEYED against the elevation's own stations. Section bays stay hand-dressed with Decorate Selection: a bay is one station's worth of cave seen end-on, so there is no station cloud to reason against and no outside that geometry can find.

It dresses traced walls — what you drew with Feature Trace — not the generated wall runs on the CTRL- layers, which are redrawn from the survey every time the cave is drawn and would lose their stone with each redraw. Import a survey and turn the switch on before tracing anything and it will tell you it found nothing to dress.

When it refuses

  • Flowstone, rimstone and slope spines live on CTRL-SHAPE-SPINE, off by default. Switch it on to reshape those edges.
