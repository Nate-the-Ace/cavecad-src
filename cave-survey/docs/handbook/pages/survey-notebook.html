The shot ladder, with the trip's date, team and declination above it.


Type the trip's header and shots; the status line keeps count as you go.

When you reach for it

Every time shots come out of a cave. This is where survey data enters a drawing, and where a trip is corrected afterwards.

Steps

  1. Open the panel and type the shot ladder: station names, distance, azimuth, inclination, LRUD, and the note in the margin.
  2. Press Scan to put the page you are typing off under the ladder in the same dock, on a splitter you drag. Under rather than beside, because a shot row is wide and squeezing it is what costs you a wrong number.
  3. Watch closures, stats and warnings update as you type.
  4. Draw writes it into the drawing in one undo step. Adding a trip redraws only that trip; anything that moves existing stations — a corrected declination, a re-closed loop — takes the full redraw. Redraw All in the ... menu forces it.

Watching the page draw itself

As you type, the page is drawn over the map as a ghost: orange shot lines, blue dashed walls from the LRUD, and a grey dashed line wherever loop closure has moved a leg appreciably. Nothing is added to the drawing — no entity, no undo step, nothing to save. Draw is still the only thing that commits.

It is there because the status line's numbers are a poor way to catch a typing mistake: a transposed azimuth is a plausible bearing and a metric distance on a page of feet is a plausible distance. Both are obvious the moment the passage is drawn.

The ghost lands exactly where Draw will put the shots — same tie-in, same anchor, same adjustment — because both ask the same code where the page goes. Only the stations on this page are ghosted; the cave already drawn around them is left alone.

The Preview button turns it off, for typing over a dense part of a finished map. What you leave it as is what you get next time.

LRUD: blank is not the same as P

Leave an LRUD cell blank and you are saying nobody measured it: the wall on that side is unknown, and the map breaks its wall line at that station rather than guessing across the gap.

Type P and you are saying the party looked and found no wall that way — open passage. That is a reading, not a gap, so the wall either side carries straight through, and the 3D passage tube keeps its shape instead of pinching shut at the station.

This matters most at tie-ins, where the shot cuts across the passage rather than running down it and both side readings come back open. Write P on those and the passage stays continuous through the tie-in.

Declination

The Notebook owns it: estimate declination from the survey date and the cave's location (IGRF), pin that location to a station as the drawing's geo anchor, and correct a trip's declination after the fact — the drawing rotates around the fix.

Correcting a trip

Load from drawing revises that trip by identity, so fixing its date or team does not fork a duplicate. Edit this trip... in the ... menu is the narrower correction: name, date, team, instruments, with nothing moved and no redraw. Declination is not among them — changing it rotates the plan, so it stays in Declination.

Try it on Truitt

Open the ... menu, Edit this trip..., and correct a trip's date. Then check the trip count is unchanged: not forking a duplicate is the whole reason that path exists.

When it refuses

  • Delete... refuses outright when a later trip ties into a station only this one reaches — that trip would be left with nowhere to start.
  • Deleting asks separately what to do with linework bound to the trip, and leaves it unbound by default.
