Lesson one, three steps ticked. Each checkbox is a numbered step from that lesson's page.


Tick the steps of a lesson as you do them, then move on. Your place is remembered.

When you reach for it

The first time you open CaveCAD, and every time you come back to the course. It is the panel that says what to do next; the rest of the handbook answers questions.

Steps

  1. Cave Survey > Start Here. It opens on the first lesson you have not finished.
  2. Work down the checkboxes. Each one is a numbered step from that lesson's page, so there is one set of words, not two.
  3. Read this lesson opens the whole page in the handbook — what you should see, and what it means when it goes wrong.
  4. Next > when the count says every step is done.
  5. Start over forgets every tick in every lesson. Nothing you have drawn is touched.

What it does not do

It never ticks a step for you. Deciding from the drawing that a wall has been traced well enough is a judgement, and a panel that ticked a step a student had not understood would teach them the ticks mean nothing.

Try it on Truitt

Set the teaching cave up first — Teaching Cave > Set up — then open Start Here and do lesson one. It is all reading and looking; nothing is drawn.
