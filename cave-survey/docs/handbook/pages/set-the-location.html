Set the cave's location, and what the datum will do to you

One station, pinned to a real position, is what lets the suite estimate declination, fetch surface data, and put the cave at its true elevation.

Steps

  1. Survey Notebook > Declination.
  2. Give the position and pick the station it belongs to — A1, the entrance, by convention.
  3. Accept when it offers to store that as the drawing's geo anchor.
  4. Estimate declination from the survey date (IGRF) rather than typing a remembered number.

The datum trap

A cave surveyed to an absolute datum has its entrance at a real elevation — say 780 ft — and every station is measured against that. Anything that treats a missing elevation as zero rebases the whole cave to sea level: the map still looks right, and every depth on it is wrong by 780 ft.

So when a tool asks for an elevation, give it the real one. An elevation left blank is not a neutral answer.

Privacy

The location is the one fact about a cave that must not travel by accident. It never appears on a map drawn by these tools, and it is stripped from a sanitized package and from a teaching cave. Handing over a raw working drawing hands over the entrance.
