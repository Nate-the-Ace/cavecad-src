Station, shot, splay, LRUD

The four words every other page assumes.

Station

A named point in the cave — a flag, a paint mark, a scratch on a rock. A1, A2, B7. By convention A1 is the entrance. A station is a place you can go back to; that is what makes it worth naming.

Shot

One measurement between two stations: distance along the tape, azimuth (the compass bearing), and inclination (how steeply it climbs or drops). Three numbers and two names are a shot, and a survey is a chain of them.

Splay

A shot from a station to a wall, floor or ceiling rather than to another station. It is a measured wall hit, aimed where the caver pointed, so a splay into a dome shows the void instead of denting the passage.

LRUD

Four distances recorded at a station: left, right, up, down. They say how wide and how tall the passage is there. LRUD is what gives a centreline a body — the walls drawn from survey alone, the cross section cut from the survey, and the passage in the 3D view all come from it.

LRUD is a rough box; splays are the detail. Where both exist the suite uses both.
