Surface Data

Put what is above the cave into the drawing: a georeferenced aerial photograph, surface elevation contours, or both. sd

When you reach for it

When the question is where the cave sits under the ground — a second entrance, a sink, how much rock is over the big room.

Steps

  1. Cave Survey > Surface Data. Two checkboxes, both on by default: aerial imagery (USGS NAIP) and contours (USGS 3DEP, labelled in the drawing's unit).
  2. Both need the drawing's geo anchor: an existing Geo Reference, a station named A1 (the entrance, by convention), or a single selected station point.

The contours are one block

Every contour line and elevation label a fetch draws goes into a single block, inserted once, with its grip on the entrance station. Click any contour and the whole surface selects, so it can be dragged, switched off or deleted in one go without a window-select that also catches the cave — and it moves about the entrance, not about some far-off drawing origin. Their layers still work as they always did: CTRL-CONTOUR and CTRL-CONTOUR-MAJOR.

Fetching again replaces the set completely — the old linework and the old block are deleted first, so nothing from an earlier fetch is left behind.

When it refuses

  • With no anchor at all it refuses outright rather than fetching anything. Set one first from Survey Notebook > Declination, which offers to store it.

Privacy

An aerial photograph is the cave's location baked into a picture. It never reaches a plotted sheet, and it never travels in a sanitized package.
