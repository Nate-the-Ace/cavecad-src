Put aerial imagery and contours under the cave

What is above the passage: a georeferenced aerial photograph and surface elevation contours.

Set the location first

  1. Survey Notebook > Declination.
  2. Give the cave's position and pin it to a station — A1, the entrance, by convention. The Notebook offers to store it as the drawing's geo anchor.

Then fetch

  1. Cave Survey > Surface Data.
  2. Both boxes are on by default: imagery (USGS NAIP) and contours (USGS 3DEP, labelled in the drawing's unit).

Selecting them afterwards

The contours are drawn as one block, inserted once, gripped at the entrance station. Click any line and the whole surface selects together, so it moves, hides or deletes in one go. Fetching again wipes the previous set — linework and block — before drawing the new one.

What else it leaves you

The elevation grid stays beside the drawing, and the ground elevation at the entrance is stored on the anchor station. Those two are what let the 3D View show the cave standing under its own hillside — switch Terrain on there once this has run.

When it refuses

  • With no anchor — no Geo Reference, no station named A1, no single selected station — it refuses outright rather than fetching anything. Set the location, then run it.

Privacy

An aerial photograph is the cave's location baked into a picture. It never reaches a plotted sheet and never travels in a sanitized package — but it does live in your working drawing, so a drawing handed over raw hands over the entrance.

The elevation grid and the entrance's ground elevation are the same kind of fact: a ground height plus a photograph names one hillside in one county. Both are stripped from a sanitized drawing and kept out of a sanitized package, alongside the coordinates.
