See where a loop error actually happened

"Closes at 2.4%" is a number about a whole loop. The arrows say which stations moved, how far, and which way.

Steps

  1. Cave Survey > Loop Errors. The CTRL-CLOSURE layer is switched on for you.
  2. Read each arrow: from where the raw survey put the station to where it is drawn, coloured in four bands from "within a good tape read" to "resurvey this loop".
  3. Read the caption. It states the exaggeration the arrows are drawn at, with the real numbers — a tenth of a foot on a cave 1400 ft across is a hundredth of a pixel, and an exaggeration nobody is told about is a falsified map.
  4. Decide: a red arrow at one station usually means a bad shot into it, not a bad loop.
  5. Switch CTRL-CLOSURE off before plotting.

When it refuses

  • No loops means nothing to draw, and it says so. Not a fault — but nothing in that survey is checking anything else.
  • With adjustment off the arrows reverse and mean "where this station would move to". The caption says which you are looking at.
