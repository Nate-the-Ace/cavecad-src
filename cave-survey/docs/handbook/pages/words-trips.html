Trips, and revising one

A cave is one drawing holding numbered trips. A trip is a day underground: a date, a team, and the shots they brought back.

Why trips matter after the day

Everything drawn carries the trip whose ground it sits on — walls, shaped lines, symbols, sections — derived from the nearest station, never defaulted. So a trip can be corrected later and its work follows, instead of being left behind.

When a correction bends the cave — a trip's declination, a fixed point, a loop closure that spreads error — your traced walls bend with it, each vertex following its local stretch. Symbols follow too: each is carried to where the ground under it went and turned by how far that ground turned, but never resized, because a stalactite's size is a drawing convention and not survey data. Notes and callouts slide and stay upright, so writing is always read the right way up. A callout moves as one piece on the tip of its leader, so the arrow stays on the feature it points at and the note keeps the layout you gave it. Cross sections and floor-elevation labels are tied to the leg they were cut on, so they follow that leg exactly: a traced section's sketch moves with its station and its leader is re-aimed to meet it. A shaped line's ornament and an area's pattern are regenerated from the line or boundary they belong to.

What counts as "near enough to follow." A drawn item is tied to the stations around it. One that sits in the middle of a wide room, well away from every station, is tied to the nearest few instead, up to ten times the usual station spacing away, and follows those. Past that it is not tied to anything, so it cannot follow; the revision report then names those items by layer, with a count, instead of leaving you to spot them on the printout. Move such an item nearer the survey or redraw it there, then revise again.

Revising

Load a trip from the drawing, change it, draw it again: the same trip is revised by identity. Retyping it by hand matches on date and team instead, so correcting a date that way forks a duplicate and the cave is drawn twice.

Deleting

Deleting a trip removes its shots, marks and legs and renumbers every later trip — trip ids are stamped into the drawing, including on your traced linework. It asks by name, asks separately what to do with linework bound to it, and refuses outright when a later trip ties into a station only this one reaches.
