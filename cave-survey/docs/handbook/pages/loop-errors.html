Loop Errors

Draw the loop closure error where it actually happened: one arrow at every station the adjustment moved. le

When you reach for it

When Survey Stats says something like "closes at 2.4%" and you want to know where. That number is not a grade you passed or failed — it says walking the loop and coming back landed you 2.4% of the way you walked from where you started, and that the adjustment has since shared that difference out among the stations.

Steps

  1. Cave Survey > Loop Errors. The CTRL-CLOSURE layer is switched on for you.
  2. Read the arrows: each runs from where the raw survey put a station to where it is drawn, coloured in four bands from "within a good tape read" to "resurvey this loop".
  3. Each loop is labelled with its own miss and percentage.
  4. Switch CTRL-CLOSURE off before plotting. This is a diagnostic drawn over a map, never map ink.

The exaggeration

A tenth of a foot on a cave 1400 ft across is a hundredth of a pixel, so the arrows are exaggerated by one factor chosen from the data — printed on the drawing beside them, with the real numbers. An exaggeration nobody is told about is a falsified map.

When it refuses

  • A survey with no loops is told so plainly. Not a fault — most caves start that way — but it does mean nothing in the survey is checking anything else.
  • With adjustment switched off the arrows point the other way and mean "where this station would move to". The caption says which you are looking at.
