Layers: what the map is saying

A cave map's layers are not filing. Each one is a claim about the line on it, and the reader is meant to be able to tell them apart.

Three things a layer carries

  • Colour — what it is. A wall, a ledge, water, breakdown.
  • Weight — how important it is. A passage outline is heavier than the ornament inside it.
  • Linetype — how certain it is. Solid was measured; dashed was inferred.

Solid against dashed is where a beginner reader goes wrong, because it looks like a drawing choice. It is a statement about how much of the map was surveyed.

Twins

The profile and section layers are derived from their plan row, never hand-copied — so a wall is the same colour and weight whichever view it is drawn in. Where you draw decides which of them you land on; nothing to pick.

When a drawing looks wrong

A drawing keeps the layer appearance it was born with, so a cave started before a palette change stays on the old one. Repair Drawing's layers pass rewrites it.
