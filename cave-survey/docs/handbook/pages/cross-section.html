Cross Section

A section across the passage, cut from the survey or traced from your field book. cxs

When you reach for it

Where the plan cannot say what the passage is shaped like — a canyon, a keyhole, a room with a balcony.

The three routes

  1. Cut it from the survey (the default): two clicks — a point on the passage, then where the section goes. The outline is lofted from the two neighbouring stations' own LRUD and splays in 3D, so a cut can be taken anywhere along a leg rather than only at a station. The caption states the scale and how far the cut is from the nearer station that fed it: a cut beside a station is nearly a measurement, one midway between distant stations is an interpolation, and the section says which it is.
  2. Trace a scanned section: opens a staging bay — a locked frame parked clear of the plan holding the scan and a dashed ghost of the station's own computed LRUD outline. Scale the scan onto the ghost and trace by hand. While the bay is open the dock offers Capture (sweeps whatever is traced inside the frame into its own block, leaders it home to the station, and proposes a placement clear of the plan's walls — press Enter to take it or click somewhere else) and Cancel (removes the frame, scan and ghost, leaving what you traced where it is).
  3. Reopen a section I already traced: puts a placed section's linework back loose in a fresh bay at the scale it was traced at, and removes the placed block so the section exists in only one place at a time. Capture puts it back.

The station list is offered in walk order — the order the survey visited them — and opens already sitting on the station after the last one that carries a section, so sketching a run of sections down a passage walks forward on its own. That is read off the drawing, not remembered between runs, so it is still right after a restart or on someone else's copy of the cave; skip a station and you are brought back to the gap once you reach the end.

Each section is its own block: it drags as a unit, it can be edited without touching any other, and Draw redefines it in place as the survey changes without moving where you put it.

Try it on Truitt

Trace one scanned section end to end. Two questions worth asking yourself: was it obvious how to finish, and how to get out without finishing?

When it refuses

  • A station with no cuttable LRUD still opens the bay — with no ghost, and it says so, rather than failing.
  • Capture with nothing traced yet is refused with an explanation, not an empty block.
