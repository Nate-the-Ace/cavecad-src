# Shaped lines move into Feature Trace, and pick their own side

Date: 2026-09-07
Status: built and published in 0.9.62.0

## What was wrong

Two things, reported together: "the shaped linetypes workflow needs
work. I want a thumbnail style button to initiate the command, and think
of a good way that we can adjust the sidedness of the shapes without
having to select and issue a reverse command."

**The side was a fact about the caver's hand, not about the cave.** A
shaped line's ornament landed on `side = +1`, which means "right of the
direction you dragged". Nobody knows which way they dragged once the
stroke is finished, so the workflow was: draw it, look at it, notice the
hachures are on the wrong side, select the line (or its ticks), run Flip
Shaped Side. Four steps to answer a question the caver could have
answered by pointing.

**Six buttons lived on their own toolbar.** A caver tracing a cave draws
walls, ledges and flowstone in the same breath, with the same drag, and
had to know that six of those live on a different bar with different
icons.

## What was built

### The side is picked by pointing

`ShapedLinesRun` gained a third state. Press and drag as before; on
RELEASE the spine is fitted and the view decided, and the action enters
`PickingSide` instead of committing. The whole feature -- spine and
ornament, generated by the same `CsShapeLine.buildDecor` that makes the
real thing -- follows the cursor from one side of the line to the other.
Click to place it on that side; Escape drops it, leaving nothing behind
and no undo step to step over.

`CsShapeLine.sideForPoint(pts, closed, point)` is the maths, and it is
pure: nearest segment (clamped to its ends, so pointing past the end of
a ledge still works), then the sign of the cross product of that
segment's tangent with the vector to the point. A point exactly on the
line answers null and the caller keeps the side it had, so a cursor
crossing the spine cannot flicker the ornament.

**A closed pit ignores the cursor entirely** and always answers inward.
Hachures point into the hole, and a caver hovering outside the loop
means the same pit.

The frame -- plan, elevation or section -- is decided at the RELEASE,
not at the click. Pointing at the low side moves the cursor off the
stroke, and often out of the band or bay it was drawn in; a ledge traced
in an elevation must not become plan linework because the caver reached
outside to point.

### The tiles live in Feature Trace

Nathan's call, and the right one: Feature Trace already IS "arm a
feature, drag it freehand, and where you drag picks the view". A shaped
line is that with ornament attached. The panel grew a second group,
`SHAPED_ROWS`, whose tiles arm `ShapedLinesRun` instead of
`FeatureTraceRun`; the cursor readout, the run selector and the view
routing are shared and needed no changes.

Every tile in the panel -- both groups -- now carries a picture:

- a plain feature draws a sample stroke in its layer's own colour, and
  dashed when the layer's linetype is, read through `CsLayers.styleOf`
  so a tile cannot disagree with the map;
- a shaped tile generates its own ornament through `CsShapeLine.prims`,
  on a sample stroke long enough to show FOUR repeats at the style's own
  spacing. Stretching the sample rather than squeezing the ornament
  keeps the tile honest about density.

`Core/CsTileArt.js` is the shared painter, extracted because the Symbol
Palette had the same twenty lines and a second copy is a second place
for a tile to stop matching what it names.

### The Cave Lines toolbar is retired

Gone, with its `EAction.getToolBar` call. Every typed command survives
(`lgf`, `lgc`, `pte`, `fst`, `rst`, `slp`, `shf`, `shs`, `shl`) and
still reaches the same actions, so notes and muscle memory keep working.
Flip Shaped Side stays: a line drawn before this existed, or one whose
drop turns out to be the other way, still needs it.

## Verified

- 26/26 headless suites, including 11 new unit assertions for
  `sideForPoint` (opposite sides above and below, the same PLACE giving
  opposite tags on a reversed stroke, past-the-end, a hooked line where
  the nearest segment decides, closed loops, degenerate input) and 14
  for `CsTileArt`.
- `tests/section_sketch_run.js` drives the two-step action in order and
  asserts the view is still decided by the stroke, not by where the side
  was picked.
- Live in the GUI: release enters the pick state with nothing placed;
  hovering below the line gives one side and above it the other, with
  the preview redrawing; the click commits on the pointed side with its
  decoration; Escape leaves the drawing untouched; a pit's side is
  stable wherever the cursor goes; 7/7 plain and 6/6 shaped tiles render.

## Still owed

A dry run by a person: does pointing at the low side feel like the
answer, or like an extra click? The click was chosen over a modifier
because it is visible -- the ornament is on screen, on that side, before
anything is committed -- but only drawing a passage's worth of ledges
says whether that trade is right.


## Addendum: the preview flickered (0.9.63.0)

Reported the same day: "the shaped line preview was flickering and was
hard to see when trying to choose a side. it was flickering while i
moved the mouse, even on the same side."

**QCAD clears the preview between mouse events.** The move handler only
re-added it when the side CHANGED, which meant a caver holding the
cursor on one side -- exactly when they are trying to look at it -- got
one frame of feature and then nothing until they crossed the line. Every
move re-adds it now.

**The obvious optimisation is a segfault.** Rebuilding the feature per
move costs 20-25 ms on a 400 ft ledge, so the first fix cached the built
ENTITIES and re-added those: 0.13 ms, and it killed the application on
the second preview, inside `RTransaction::addObject`. The first preview
takes ownership of the entities. This is now an engine truth worth
carrying: **a preview operation consumes the entities you give it;
never hand the same objects to two of them.**

What is cached instead is the spine SAMPLE, which is plain data.
Measured on a 188-point spine: walking the spine 20 ms, the ornament
maths 0.3 ms, entity construction 1.5 ms, spline fit 1.1 ms. So
`CsShapeLine.buildDecor` gained an optional `sample` argument, the pick
state walks the spine once, and every move generates a fresh feature
through the same function that commits the real one -- 3.5 ms per move
at 57 spine points, 9 ms at 140.

**Visibility.** The preview drew in the layer's own peru, on a white
sheet, often over a grey scan. It draws in QCAD's measurement colour at
`Weight050` now: the preview is a question being asked, not the drawing,
and it should not have to compete with the map to be seen. The committed
feature keeps its proper layer appearance.
